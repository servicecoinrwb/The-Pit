What this is

Open outcry ran on a stepped octagon like the one above. You stood on a step, shouted a price, and someone shouted back. The whole thing worked because there was always somebody on the other side.

There is nobody here. Instead a pool of deposited capital is counterparty to every position on the desk — traders who win are paid out of it, traders who lose pay into it. Depositors are, collectively, the house.

No book, no matching

Prices come from an oracle, not from bids meeting offers. Size is available at the mark until the pool runs out of room to honour it.

Funding goes to the pool

When longs outweigh shorts the pool is net short, so it collects from longs directly. Same economics as a book, none of the distribution machinery.

The feed can shut the desk

A price older than five minutes stops counting as a price and trading halts. A dead feeder closes the floor rather than settling anyone against a stale print.

Two sources must agree

ETH, BTC, SOL, XRP and gold are checked against a second exchange before publishing. When they disagree, nothing is published and the desk goes quiet. The index and energy futures have no free second source, and the interface says so rather than hiding it.

Open positions pay rent

Funding nets to nothing on a balanced book and fees only arrive when someone trades. A borrow fee scaled to how much of the pool is committed is what pays for carrying risk in between.

Something watches for liquidations

Anyone can liquidate an underwater position, but a promise nobody enforces is not a promise. A keeper sweeps every forty-five seconds so the maintenance floor is a real floor.

Stops live on the chain

A front end cannot close your position for you, so stop losses and take profits are stored with the position itself. Anyone may execute one that has been crossed and takes a small cut for it — that reward is what makes them get serviced rather than merely permitted.

Take some of it off

Closing part of a position leaves the rest exactly as it was: same entry, same leverage, same liquidation price, funding still accruing from where it already was. Only size and margin shrink.

Stops beat liquidations

When a position is past both its stop and its maintenance floor, the keeper fires the stop. That returns what is left to the trader; a liquidation would hand most of it to the pool. The instruction wins.

What it will not do

Every design here bought something by giving something up. The trades are worth stating plainly rather than discovering them with a position open.

Profit is capped at half your size.

That exact amount is set aside in the pool the moment you open, so a winner is always payable. The cost is that a runner stops earning past the cap. A pool that promises unbounded payouts cannot reserve against them, and an unreserved promise is the one that breaks.

A halted market traps a position.

Closing needs a live price, because settling against a stale one robs whichever side the staleness favours. Margin can always be added, so a position can be defended through a closure even when it cannot be exited.

Four markets run on delayed, unchecked data.

US30, SPX, NAS and WTI track futures from a single free source, typically ten to fifteen minutes behind. Nobody offers a second free feed for any of them, so unlike the crypto markets these prices are published without being checked against anything. Anyone holding a real-time terminal knows where they are before this desk does. Academic on a testnet; disqualifying anywhere real.

A stop fills where the oracle is, not where you set it.

Price gapping straight through a stop fills past it, and that difference is yours. Paying out at the trigger instead would put the gap on the pool, and depositors did not agree to cover the worst moment of someone else's trade. A halted market fires nothing at all, so a weekend gap settles on the reopen rather than at Friday's level.

A position got permanently stuck, and that is why the desk was rebuilt.

The first version counted the same reserved capital twice — once in the desk, once in the pool — and payouts moved one without the other. Once they disagreed, closing reverted, and because liquidation unwinds through the same path, the position could not be liquidated either. A live trade hit it and its margin is still in the old contract. The pool is now the only counter that decides anything, and every release and payout clamps to what it actually holds rather than reverting. On a testnet this cost some worthless tokens. It is the kind of thing that only surfaces when a real position lands on it.

The money is not money.

Collateral is a test token anyone can mint, on a testnet, worthless on purpose. The Arc faucet gives a dollar a day, which cannot seed a pool deep enough for any number here to mean anything.
